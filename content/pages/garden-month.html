August in the Garden

August brings peak summer heat and rain here in St. Pete (Zone 10a/9b). High humidity and abundant afternoon downpours create ideal conditions for rapid tropical foliage and outdoor growth. Use this guide to manage hydration, tropical pest prevention, and late-summer plantings.

What to Plant

  • Heat-tolerant crops: Plant Everglades tomatoes, sweet potatoes, seminole pumpkin, okra, and southern peas that thrive in Florida's summer heat.
  • Subtropical & Tropical Fruits: Plant container-grown tropical fruit trees (mango, papaya, passionfruit, and banana) while soil temperatures and humidity remain warm.
  • Pollinator Attractors: Sow or transplant porterweed, milkweed, and pentas to support local pollinators during summer blooms.

Summer Maintenance & Pruning

  • Monitor drainage for potted tropicals so heavy afternoon rains do not cause root waterlogging.
  • Prune leggy growth on fast-growing vines and tropical shrubs to maintain shape.
  • Apply mulch around root zones to retain soil moisture and shield tender feeder roots from midday heat.

Indoor Plant Care

  • Increase humidity for tropical houseplants by grouping pots or using pebble trays.
  • Check for pests like spider mites and scale as temperatures rise; treat early with insecticidal soap.
  • Begin light fertilization with a balanced houseplant food.
  • Rotate pots weekly to ensure even growth toward the light.
